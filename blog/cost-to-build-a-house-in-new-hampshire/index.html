Homes with multiple stories require additional structural engineering, stairs, and framing beyond a single-story build of equal square footage. Roof design shifts the cost, since a complex hip roof or multiple-gable roof requires additional materials and labor beyond a standard gable design. Architectural features, including large windows, vaulted ceilings, and custom trim work, add material and installation costs. Custom specifications, including non-standard door and window sizes, require special ordering that raises material pricing above stock options.

Can a More Complex Design Increase Construction Costs?

Yes, a more complex design increases construction costs. Additional materials become necessary when a design includes irregular angles, multiple rooflines, or custom architectural features. Labor hours extend on complex builds, since skilled trades require additional time to execute non-standard framing or finish details. Engineering requirements increase with design complexity, particularly for multi-story homes or structures with large open spans. Construction work slows on intricate designs, since crews move through custom details carefully rather than repetitive standard layouts. A simplified design keeps construction costs lower by reducing engineering, labor, and material complexity throughout the build.
